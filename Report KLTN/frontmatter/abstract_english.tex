\begin{center}
{\fontsize{16pt}{19.2pt}\selectfont \textbf{BUILDING A REINFORCEMENT LEARNING MODEL FOR OPTIMIZING REORDER POLICY IN MULTI-WAREHOUSE INVENTORY MANAGEMENT}\par}
\vspace{0.5cm}
{\fontsize{16pt}{19.2pt}\selectfont \textbf{ABSTRACT}\par}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.2cm}

Inventory control in a retail network with many warehouses and many products is
a sequential decision problem under uncertainty: holding, stockout, ordering and
overflow costs must be balanced while a customer service level is maintained.
Classical policies such as EOQ, $(s, S)$ and the Newsvendor model assume
stationary demand, fixed lead times and unconstrained storage, and lose their
optimality when these assumptions are violated.

This thesis models $10$ warehouses $\times$ $30$ products as $300$ agents, each
choosing the replenishment quantity of one warehouse--product pair from local
observations, with the products of a warehouse sharing a finite capacity. The
agents are trained with Independent PPO under parameter sharing (PS-IPPO), using
a local factored reward per pair, per-pair reward normalization, and an $85\%$
service-level target implemented as a soft constraint.

The simulation uses the real demand of the M5 Walmart dataset ($1{,}941$ days),
split into training, validation and test periods, with stochastic lead times of
one to three days. PS-IPPO is compared with three classical policies tuned on
the validation period, over several training seeds and with paired statistical
tests.

With the main model ($10{,}000$ episodes $\times$ $3$ seeds), at the operating
point each method selects for itself, PS-IPPO has a total cost close to the best
classical policies --- $1.7\%$ below $(s, S)$ and $17.8\%$ below EOQ, and on par
with the versions tuned per demand-scale group --- while reaching a service level
about $4$--$5$ percentage points higher ($90.9\%$ versus $85.8$--$86.2\%$). When
the classical policies are forced to a comparable service level ($\approx 90\%$)
they cost $17.3$--$25.7\%$ more than PS-IPPO (an upper bound, because the
parameter grid is coarse), and EOQ cannot reach it. Under stable demand,
group-tuned classical policies match or beat PS-IPPO; its advantage is
concentrated in volatile demand periods (event days, weekends, demand shocks).
Parameter sharing maintains performance for medium- and high-demand products,
but the service level of low-demand products falls short of the target ($28.7\%$
of pairs below $85\%$). The contributions are a PS-IPPO framework with shared
warehouse capacity on real retail data, an evaluation protocol that separates
operating cost, service level and the training signal, and a simulation
application that proposes purchase orders from the trained model.

\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} inventory management; multi-agent reinforcement
learning; IPPO; parameter sharing; M5.
